Predictive Modeling

Selecting the Right Regression Model

While Week 8 focused on comparing just two variables (Correlation & Simple Linear Regression), real-world veterinary medicine involves predicting outcomes based on multiple compounding risk factors.

The Dependent Variable (Outcome) dictates the model.

  • Continuous Outcome: (e.g., Blood Pressure, Weight, Clearance Time) → Use Linear Regression.
  • Categorical Binary Outcome: (e.g., Disease Present vs. Absent, Dead vs. Alive) → Use Logistic Regression.
  • 1 Predictor: Simple Regression (Week 8).
  • 2+ Predictors: Multiple Regression (Week 9).

Multiple Linear Regression

Predicting Continuous Outcomes

2+ Predictor Variables (IVs) 1 Continuous Outcome (DV)

VETMED EXAMPLE:

Predicting Anesthesia Recovery Time (in minutes) based on the combined effects of the patient's Weight (kg), Age (years), and Liver Enzyme Levels (ALT in U/L).

JAMOVI EXECUTION:

  • Regression > Linear Regression
  • Dependent Variable: The Continuous Outcome.
  • Covariates: Continuous predictors.
  • Factors: Categorical predictors (e.g., Sex, Breed).

DO NOT USE WHEN:

  • The outcome you are trying to predict is a "Yes or No" result (e.g., Will the patient survive?).

Binary Logistic Regression

Predicting Probabilities & Risks

1 or More Predictor Variables (IVs) 1 Binary Outcome (DV) (Yes/No)

VETMED EXAMPLE:

Predicting the likelihood of a horse developing Equine Gastric Ulcer Syndrome (EGUS) (Present/Absent) based on their Training Intensity (hours/week), NSAID Administration (Yes/No), and Forage Diet Percentage.

JAMOVI EXECUTION:

  • Regression > 2 Outcomes Binomial (Logistic)
  • Dependent Variable: The Binary Outcome (Ensure level 1 is the "event" you want to predict).
  • Covariates/Factors: Your continuous and categorical predictors.

Model Evaluation & Diagnostics

Key Metrics to Check

1. Model Fit (R²)

  • Found in Linear Regression. Represents the percentage of variance in the Dependent Variable explained by your model. (e.g., R² = 0.75 means 75% of anesthesia recovery time is explained by Weight, Age, and ALT).

2. Multicollinearity (VIF)

  • Rule: Predictor variables should not be highly correlated with "each other".
  • Check the Collinearity Statistics box in Jamovi.
  • If VIF (Variance Inflation Factor) is > 5.0, you have a multicollinearity problem. (e.g., Including both "Weight in kg" and "Weight in lbs" as separate predictors).

3. Odds Ratios (OR)

  • Found in Logistic Regression. Shows the change in odds of the outcome occurring.
  • OR > 1: Increases the risk (e.g., NSAID use increases ulcer risk).
  • OR < 1: Decreases the risk (Protective factor).
