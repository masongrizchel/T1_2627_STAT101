Bivariate Relationships

Direction

Evaluating whether variables move in the same (positive) or opposite (negative) directions.

Strength

Measuring the magnitude of the relationship on a scale from -1.0 to +1.0.

Causation Trap

Reinforcing the principle that correlation does not establish a cause-and-effect relationship.

Pearson Correlation Coefficient

Continuous Data

Both variables must be measured at the continuous (interval or ratio) level.

Linearity

The relationship between the variables must form a linear trajectory on a scatterplot.

Parametric

Assumes bivariate normality and the absence of significant outliers.

Spearman's Rank-Order Correlation

Ordinal Data

The non-parametric alternative used when one or both variables are ranked data.

Monotonicity

Evaluates variables that move together but not necessarily at a constant linear rate.

Robustness

Highly resistant to outliers and does not require normal distribution assumptions.

Simple Linear Regression

Predictive Modeling

Using a known independent predictor variable to forecast an unknown dependent outcome.

The Equation

Establishing the line of best fit using the formula: Y = a + bX.

R-Squared

Interpreting the coefficient of determination to understand explained variance.

Visual Diagnostics & Assumptions

Scatterplots

Generating visual plots in Jamovi prior to executing correlational tests.

Homoscedasticity

Checking that the spread of residuals remains roughly constant across the regression line.

Problem-Based Lab Exercises

Correlation Matrices

Executing Pearson and Spearman tests simultaneously to compare output tables.

Model Interpretation

Translating regression coefficients and R-squared values into APA reporting.
