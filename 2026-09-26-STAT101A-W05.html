Hypothesis Testing: t-Tests in Biostatistics

The Goal

Determine if differences between means are statistically significant or just random chance.

The Data

Requires a continuous dependent variable and categorical independent variables.

Biostatistics Focus

Essential for clinical trials, treatment efficacy, and physiological comparisons.

Types of t-Tests

One-Sample

Compares a single sample mean against a known population mean or standard value.

Independent Samples

Compares the means of two entirely distinct, unrelated groups (e.g., Control vs. Treatment).

Paired (Dependent)

Compares means from the same group at different times (e.g., Pre-test vs. Post-test).

Checking Equality of Variances

The Assumption

Independent t-tests assume both groups have roughly equal variances (spread).

Levene's Test

If p > 0.05, variances are equal (Assumption Met). Use Student's t.

Violation Fix

If p < 0.05, variances are unequal. Switch to Welch's t-test in Jamovi.

Interpreting the Output

p-value

Tells you IF there is a significant difference (usually p < 0.05).

Effect Size (Cohen's d)

Tells you HOW LARGE the difference is, regardless of sample size.

Cohen's d Scale

0.2 = Small | 0.5 = Medium | 0.8 = Large effect.
