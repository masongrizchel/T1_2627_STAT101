Time, Covariates & Reliability

Repeated Measures ANOVA

Moving beyond the standard One-Way ANOVA. Use this when you are measuring the exact same patients multiple times across different timepoints or conditions.

Tracking Change Over Time

1 Within-Subjects Factor (e.g., Time) 1 Continuous Outcome (DV)

VETMED EXAMPLE:

Measuring the continuous Blood Pressure (mmHg) of a canine cohort at three distinct timepoints: Baseline, Week 2, and Week 4 after starting a new cardiac medication.

JAMOVI EXECUTION:

  • ANOVA > Repeated Measures ANOVA
  • Under Repeated Measures Factors, rename "RM Factor 1" to "Time" and add 3 levels (Baseline, W2, W4).
  • Drag your three continuous data columns into the Repeated Measures Cells.
  • Check Sphericity tests (Mauchly's) to ensure variance differences between timepoints are equal.

Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA)

A standard ANOVA tests if group means are different. An ANCOVA does the same thing, but statistically controls for a confounding variable that might be skewing your results.

Controlling the Noise

1 Categorical Grouping Variable (IV) 1 Continuous Covariate (Control) 1 Continuous Outcome (DV)

VETMED EXAMPLE:

Comparing final Weight Gain between two different diets (Supplement A vs. B), but controlling for the animal's Starting Baseline Weight (the Covariate) so it doesn't unfairly advantage one group.

JAMOVI EXECUTION:

  • ANOVA > ANCOVA
  • Dependent Variable: Final Weight Gain (Continuous).
  • Fixed Factors: Diet Type (Categorical).
  • Covariates: Starting Baseline Weight (Continuous).

Reliability & Agreement

Before analyzing clinical data, you must prove your measurement tools and your clinical raters are actually consistent and trustworthy.

Inter-Rater Reliability (IRR)

VETMED EXAMPLE:

Two different veterinary radiologists grade the same 50 spinal X-rays for severity (Grades 1-4). IRR (specifically Cohen's Kappa) measures how often they agree beyond simple random chance.

  • Interpretation: Kappa ranges from 0 to 1. A score > 0.80 indicates excellent agreement between the two veterinarians.

Cronbach's Alpha (α)

VETMED EXAMPLE:

You design a 10-question survey for pet owners to measure their dog's "Quality of Life." Cronbach's Alpha measures internal consistency—whether all 10 questions are actually measuring the same underlying concept.

JAMOVI EXECUTION:

  • Factor > Reliability Analysis
  • Drag all 10 survey items into the box.
  • Interpretation: An α score > 0.70 means your survey is mathematically reliable and ready for clinical deployment.
